# Treasure MathStorm! (The Learning Company): release history, versions, series context and availability today

Confidence tags used below: **[MULTI]** means two or more independent sources agree. **[SINGLE]** means only one source was found. **[UNCERTAIN]** means sources conflict, the claim comes only from a search-result snippet, or it is unverified.
Research limits: MobyGames (releases, specs, credits and IP-rights pages) returned HTTP 403 on every request. The Wayback Machine could not be reached from this environment, and eBay item pages also returned 403. MobyGames facts below therefore come only from search-engine snippets or from sites that copy MobyGames data (Internet Archive descriptions, Pixelated Arcade), and are tagged that way. Nothing was downloaded, unpacked or run. Only metadata and description pages were read.

## 1. Release dates, platforms, media, versions and technical specs

### Takeaway
The Learning Company (TLC) first released Treasure MathStorm! in 1992 for DOS on 3.5" and 5.25" floppies. A Macintosh floppy version came out in about 1993, and a Windows 3.1/Mac hybrid CD-ROM (v1.0) followed in 1994. A v2.0 Windows/Mac CD appeared in about 1996–97, alongside OEM, UK, Spanish and Taiwanese editions through 1999. Exact dates conflict between databases, especially for the Mac and Windows releases.

### Inferences
- A probable version timeline:
  - 1992: DOS floppy.
  - 1992–93: Mac floppy (v1.0).
  - 1994: Win 3.1/Mac hybrid CD (v1.0).
  - About 1996–97: v2.0 Win/Mac CD, plus the 1997 OEM jewel-case CD that supports Windows 95.
  - 1996–1999: localized editions (Spanish 1996, Taiwan v1.1 1998, UK v1.1 1999).
- The UK and Taiwan "v1.1" discs (1998–99) are later than the "v2.0" disc (1996–97). This suggests the localized discs came from an older v1.x code branch, or that version numbering was not linear across regions. Neither explanation is confirmed.
- The jump from an 8088/640 KB floppy game to a 486/8 MB CD build means the CD versions were reworked substantially, with voice and SVGA, not just repackaged. The UK edition's English voice-over supports this.

### Gaps
- Floppy disk counts for the DOS 3.5" and 5.25" sets, and for the Mac floppy, were not found. MobyGames specs were blocked.
- The exact launch month for 1992 is unconfirmed. Only an IGDB-style date of 30 December 1992 was found, as a snippet.
- I could not check MobyGames' own platform dates, including its 1996 date for the Mac and Windows hybrid CD.
- Official system requirements for the 1994 v1.0 CD (Windows side) and the v2.0 CD were not found. Only the 1997 OEM requirements were.
- No evidence was found of a network, lab-pack or school edition. This is unconfirmed rather than ruled out.

## 3. Series placement: the "Treasure" series, Super Solvers and recurring characters

### Takeaway
Treasure MathStorm! is the second of four "Treasure" games, after Treasure Mountain! (1990) and before Treasure Cove! (1992) and Treasure Galaxy! (1994), and is set on the same mountain as the first game. Reference sources treat the Treasure games as a younger-child branch of TLC's Super Solvers family, but they disagree on terminology. Wikipedia calls the group "Super Seekers" in its body text and "Super Solvers" in its citations. Whether the original MathStorm box itself carried the "Super Solvers" banner was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia calls MathStorm the sequel to Treasure Mountain!, "the sixth installment of The Learning Company's Super Seekers games", and the second of the four "Treasure" games. It also says the Treasure series "is a subgroup of the company's Super Solvers series" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!). The numbering is **[SINGLE]**.
- On the same numbering, Treasure Mountain! is the third "Super Seekers" installment (1990), and Treasure Cove! is the ninth (1992) — [Wikipedia: Treasure Mountain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Mountain!); [Wikipedia: Treasure Cove!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Cove!).
- Treasure Galaxy! was released in 1994 for Windows, DOS and Mac. Wikipedia's text calls the Treasure series a subgroup of "Super Seekers" while its citation uses "Super Solvers" — [Wikipedia: Treasure Galaxy!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Galaxy!).
- The four-title series order (Mountain, MathStorm, Cove, Galaxy) appears consistently across the four Wikipedia articles above **[MULTI within Wikipedia]**.
- Evidence that MathStorm is catalogued as a "Super Solvers" title:
  - Internet Archive items titled "Super Solvers Treasure MathStorm" (1992 DOS) and "Super Solvers: Treasure MathStorm (1996)" — [IA](https://archive.org/details/msdos_Super_Solvers_Teasure_MathStorm_1992); [IA tmscd-2](https://archive.org/details/tmscd-2).
  - Wikipedia cites MobyGames' "Super Solvers Series" group for its series statement — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!).
  - Squakenet lists the game as "Super Solvers: Treasure MathStorm" **[snippet only]** — [Squakenet](https://squakenet.com/game/super-solvers-treasure-mathstorm).
- Wikipedia's TLC article says Treasure Mountain! "was originally released as a Super Solvers title" — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company). Giant Bomb titles that game "Super Solvers Treasure Mountain" — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Super_Solvers_Treasure_Mountain).
- Main Super Solvers titles named on Wikipedia: Midnight Rescue!, OutNumbered!, Challenge of the Ancient Empires!, Operation Neptune, Spellbound!, Gizmos & Gadgets! and Mission: T.H.I.N.K. — [Wikipedia: Treasure Mountain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Mountain!); [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- Later re-branding of some main-line titles: Midnight Rescue! became "Super Solvers Reading" and then "Leap Ahead! 3rd Grade", and Spellbound! became "Leap Ahead! Spelling" — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- Recurring characters:
  - **Master of Mischief**: the common antagonist across the Treasure games. In MathStorm he freezes the mountain with a weather machine and scatters the castle's treasures **[MULTI]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!); [IA description from MobyGames](https://archive.org/details/TMS_VGA); [Wikipedia: Treasure Cove!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Cove!); [Wikipedia: Treasure Galaxy!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Galaxy!).
  - Wikipedia describes him as the "counterpart of Morty Maxwell", the villain of the main Super Solvers games — [Wikipedia: Treasure Mountain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Mountain!).
  - **Super Seeker**: the player character in all four Treasure games — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!); [Wikipedia: Treasure Cove!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Cove!).
  - **Elves** appear in both Treasure Mountain! and MathStorm. **Snowbullies**, which steal money at higher ranks, are specific to MathStorm. The King is restored when all treasures are returned — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!); [IA](https://archive.org/details/TMS_VGA).
- The "Treasure Trio!" bundle contained Treasure Mountain!, Treasure Cove! and Treasure Galaxy!, but not MathStorm (cited to a 1996 PR Newswire release) **[SINGLE]** — [Wikipedia: Treasure Mountain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Mountain!).

### Inferences
- Treasure MathStorm! is best described as a Treasure-series title within TLC's wider Super Solvers family. Catalogues (MobyGames-derived, Internet Archive) attach "Super Solvers" to its name. The in-game hero is a "Super Seeker" rather than a "Super Solver", which marks the Treasure games as a separate branch for younger children.
- The "Super Seekers" label on current Wikipedia pages may be a recent editorial choice rather than period branding. Their own citations still say "Super Solvers".
- MathStorm's absence from the Treasure Trio! bundle may reflect its maths-only focus, but this is not confirmed.

### Gaps

- Release years for the main Super Solvers titles were not verified this session, because the Wikipedia "Super Solvers" article URL returned 404.

## 4. Core loops of the sibling Treasure titles (the shared formula)

### Takeaway
All four Treasure games share a three-stage structure. The player answers riddles or challenges from local creatures to earn clues and items, uses the clues to find hidden objects (treasures, gems or crystal shards), and advances through rank-based difficulty with a cosmetic prize at the end of each run **[MULTI across four Wikipedia articles]**.

### Cited Findings
- **Treasure Mountain! (1990):** The mountain has three levels, each unlocked by a key. The player nets elves to get riddle clues and magic coins, then drops coins on scenery: matching all three clues reveals the key, and matching two reveals a treasure. A ladder maze to the castle follows, then a prize in the clubhouse. Higher ranks add more treasures, harder riddles and coin-stealing elves — [Wikipedia: Treasure Mountain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Mountain!).
- **Treasure MathStorm! (1992):** The mountain has three levels again. Ice axes, ladders and catapult parts are earned by solving maths tasks such as setting clocks, balancing scales and counting crystals. Elves' riddles say how many snowballs to throw at a spot to reveal treasure. Treasures go back to the castle chest, and the reward is a toy prize in the clubhouse. Higher ranks add more treasures, harder riddles and money-stealing snowbullies. Coins can be spent in shops — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_MathStorm!); [IA](https://archive.org/details/TMS_VGA).
- **Treasure Cove! (1992):** This is an underwater side-scroller covering reading, maths and oceanography. The player collects gems to rebuild a rainbow bridge. Starfish ask questions that give three clues per stage, and shining a flashlight on a group of animals that matches two clues reveals a gem. A pufferfish plugs pipes that leak "goobies", which drain the player's light. Higher ranks add more gems, harder riddles and faster light-stealing fish — [Wikipedia: Treasure Cove!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Cove!).
- **Treasure Galaxy! (1994):** The setting is space. The player captures "sunbeams" whose riddles give cipher clues to crystals in satellites, across three orbits. Each orbit needs a 4-digit passcode earned from alien challenges such as calendar dates, tangrams, and ruler or scale measurement. Difficulty adapts, and "Disasteroids" steal "star bucks" — [Wikipedia: Treasure Galaxy!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Galaxy!).

### Inferences
- MathStorm keeps the clue-matching treasure hunt from Treasure Mountain! but changes the gating. Instead of reading riddles to find keys, the player completes maths tasks to earn climbing supplies, which makes it the most maths-focused game in the series.

### Gaps
- No contemporary (1990s) reviews of the sibling titles were consulted. These descriptions rely on Wikipedia, which in turn cites MobyGames and Abandonia.

## 5. Corporate history and rights; re-releases on modern stores or in compilations

### Takeaway
TLC, founded in 1980, published the game in 1992. SoftKey acquired TLC in a hostile takeover in late 1995 and adopted its name. Mattel bought the company in 1999 and sold it to Gores in 2000, which passed the education titles to Riverdeep in 2001. Riverdeep merged with Houghton Mifflin, which became Houghton Mifflin Harcourt (HMH) in 2007. The TLC brand now sits with HMH, though the 2018 fold-in is not well sourced. No modern digital re-release was found: the game is not on Steam, and on GOG it exists only as a Dreamlist wish.

### Cited Findings
- **1980:** TLC founded in Palo Alto by Ann McCormick, Leslie Grimm, Teri Perl and Warren Robinett — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- **April 1992:** TLC went public, in the same year MathStorm launched — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- **December 1995:** SoftKey acquired TLC for $606M in a hostile takeover, with many staff fired. SoftKey then adopted the TLC name. Wikipedia cites The New York Times, 8 December 1995 — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- **1999:** Mattel acquired TLC. Wikipedia's TLC article says 1999 without an inline citation. A snippet of Wikipedia's Mattel Interactive article refers to a 1998 agreement **[UNCERTAIN on exact date; no purchase price confirmed in sources read]** — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company); [Wikipedia: Mattel Interactive (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel_Interactive).
- **2000:** Mattel sold TLC and Mattel Interactive to Gores Technology Group at a loss. The month differs between sources: September 2000 per Wikipedia's Brøderbund article, October 2000 per MobyGames **[UNCERTAIN month; snippets]** — [Wikipedia: Mattel Interactive (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel_Interactive); [MobyGames company page (snippet)](https://www.mobygames.com/company/40/).
- **March 2001:** Gores sold the entertainment studio to Ubi Soft **[snippet]** — [Wikipedia: Mattel Interactive (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel_Interactive).
- **2001:** Riverdeep acquired the education assets for $40M in stock plus $20M in assumed liabilities, covering brands such as Reader Rabbit, Carmen Sandiego and Oregon Trail. The price comes from a snippet. The trade-press article "Gores Tech Passes Education Assets" is dated 24 September 2001, but its body was not readable **[UNCERTAIN, snippet plus headline]** — [Buyouts Insider (snippet)](https://www.buyoutsinsider.com/gores-tech-passes-education-assets/); [Private Equity International](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/gores-tech-passes-education-assets/).
- **2002:** Riverdeep also acquired the Brøderbund productivity division **[snippet]** — [MobyGames company page (snippet)](https://www.mobygames.com/company/40/).
- **2006–2007:** TLC became a brand of Houghton Mifflin (through the Riverdeep merger), which became HMH in 2007 — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company); [MobyGames (snippet)](https://mobygames.com/company/learning-company).
- **April 2018:** TLC was folded into HMH, which lists it under its Learning Technology division. Wikipedia tags this "citation needed". HMH rebranded as "HMH" in July 2024 — [Wikipedia: The Learning Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Learning_Company).
- **Modern stores, checked 2026-11-25:**

### Inferences
- The most probable current rights holder is HMH, as successor to Riverdeep and Houghton Mifflin and owner of the TLC brand. No source read here states this for Treasure MathStorm! specifically, so it should be presented as likely, not confirmed.

## 6. Present-day availability: Internet Archive, abandonware sites, used copies and emulation

### Takeaway

CD-ROM ISOs of the 1994, v2.0, UK, Spanish and Taiwanese editions are also there. No official digital sale exists. The original DOS release works in DOSBox. The Windows 3.x CD version is flagged as not working in the Internet Archive's emulator, and the Mac versions run in Basilisk II. Used boxed copies appear on eBay, but no sold-price data could be retrieved.

### Inferences
- DOSBox compatibility is effectively confirmed for the 1992 DOS release, since the Internet Archive serves five DOS items through its DOSBox-based emulator. The Windows 3.x CD editions are harder to run, as the Internet Archive's non-working flag shows. They would probably need a Windows 3.1 or 9x guest (for example DOSBox with Windows 3.1, or a virtual machine), but this was not tested.
- The Internet Archive and abandonware sites state no licence. The game has not been released into the public domain or freely licensed by its likely rights holder, HMH, and none of these sites cites rights-holder permission. Placing an item in the Internet Archive's "open_source_software" collection is an uploader choice and does not by itself mean an open licence.
- The only route that clearly involves a licensed copy is buying a used original disk or CD and running it in an emulator.

### Gaps
- No verified eBay sold-price range: eBay pages returned 403 and no WorthPoint data was found.
